## Project Overview

At **Porsche Holding** (2016–2019), Justin Güse contributed to high-profile innovation projects beyond traditional data warehousing. One of the flagship deliverables was a **VR Motion Seat experience for Formula E** - a physically reactive racing simulator that toured European events as a brand activation centrepiece.

## The Challenge

Porsche Holding needed an immersive, crowd-stopping experience that would communicate their commitment to electric mobility and motorsport innovation. Generic static displays weren't going to cut it at Formula E venues. The brief: build something that puts spectators _inside_ the car.

## The Solution

The VR Motion Seat combined:

- **Motion platform hardware** - pneumatic seat actuators synchronized to in-game physics events (braking, acceleration, cornering g-forces).
- **Oculus VR integration** - custom Unity-based Formula E circuit simulation with realistic telemetry-driven camera shake and spatial audio.
- **Robust event-ready engineering** - designed for repeated daily use by non-technical event staff across multiple European cities, with remote diagnostics and rapid reset capability.
- **Data integration** - real Formula E telemetry data used to drive motion parameters, giving the simulation authentic race feel rather than canned animations.

## Results

- Deployed at **multiple Formula E events** across Europe in 2018–2019.
- Consistently among the **highest-dwell-time activations** at each event venue.
- Demonstrated that enterprise-grade engineering rigour applies equally to consumer-facing experiential technology.

## What This Shows

This project sits at the intersection of hardware engineering, real-time 3D development, and large-scale event operations - domains far outside the typical DevOps playbook. It's evidence that Justin's technical range extends from petabyte-scale banking data pipelines to immersive motorsport experiences.
